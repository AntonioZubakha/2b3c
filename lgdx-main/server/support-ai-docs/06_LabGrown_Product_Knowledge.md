# Lab-grown diamonds — product knowledge for support

The support assistant should navigate lab-grown diamond basics confidently and answer user questions using the facts below. Do not invent details beyond this document.

---

## 1. What lab-grown diamonds are

**Lab-grown diamonds** are diamonds created in controlled manufacturing conditions rather than mined. Chemically and structurally they are **real diamonds** (pure carbon, diamond lattice). The difference from natural diamonds is **origin** only.

Key message: lab-grown diamonds are real diamonds with the same hardness and optical properties; the difference is origin. LGDEAL focuses on the B2B segment.

Platform policy: LGDEAL lists **certified only** lab-grown diamonds and focuses on high-quality inventory: clarity **VS2+** and (for the standard colorless line) color **D–G**.

---

## 2. Terminology

Common terms:
- **Lab-grown diamond**: preferred neutral term
- **Cultivated diamond**: marketing synonym
- **Synthetic diamond**: technically correct; “lab-grown” is common in jewelry

Diamond simulants (cubic zirconia, moissanite, etc.) are **not** lab-grown diamonds; they have different composition and properties.

---

## 3. Properties vs natural diamonds

In most properties, natural and lab-grown diamonds do **not** differ:

- **Composition**: carbon (C), same lattice.
- **Hardness**: 10 Mohs.
- **Optics**: brilliance and light performance depend on cut/clarity similarly.

The difference is **origin** and growth history. Visual inspection is not reliable; labs use instruments and growth markers.

**Type IIa:** many lab-grown diamonds are Type IIa (very pure). In nature Type IIa is rare (~1–2%).

---

## 4. Production methods (overview)

Two main industrial methods exist. Both use a diamond **seed** crystal.

### 4.1 HPHT (High Pressure High Temperature)

- **Idea**: mimics deep-earth conditions (high pressure + temperature).
- **Process**: carbon dissolves in a metal catalyst and deposits onto the seed; growth takes days to weeks.
- Modern HPHT can produce high-quality colorless stones.

Assistant-friendly phrasing: “HPHT is like nature — high pressure and temperature in a controlled chamber; growth takes days to weeks.”

### 4.2 CVD (Chemical Vapor Deposition)

- **Idea**: grows diamond from a carbon-containing gas onto a seed plate.
- **Process**: plasma (roughly 800–1200°C) deposits carbon layer by layer; often very clean material.
- **Time**: typically weeks.

Assistant-friendly phrasing: “CVD grows diamond from gas onto a seed plate; it’s controlled and often yields very clean stones.”

Both methods produce **real diamonds**; the method affects typical size/clarity/color and cost, not whether it is diamond.

---

## 5. Variants and the “4C”

- **By method**: HPHT and CVD (see above). The method may be listed in the certificate or product details.
- **By use**: jewelry (gem-quality) vs industrial (not the platform’s focus).
- **4C / filters**: carat, color, clarity, cut/shape.
- **Color**: colorless (D–Z scale) vs fancy colors (graded differently).

### 5.1 Shapes (examples)
Round/Brilliant, Princess, Oval, Marquise, Pear, Emerald, Asscher, Radiant, Cushion, Heart, Trillion, Fancy.

### 5.2 Clarity (quick scale)
FL, IF, VVS1/VVS2, VS1/VS2, SI1/SI2, I1–I3 (from most clean to most included). Platform policy for the standard line focuses on VS2+.

### 5.3 Color (colorless)
Colorless grading typically runs **D → Z** (increasing tint). Fancy colors use different grading systems.

---

## 6. Certification (high-level)

- Labs such as **GIA** and **IGI** certify lab-grown diamonds and indicate laboratory origin on the report.
- Reports include 4C and other measurements; many stones have a laser inscription that can match the report number.
- For apples-to-apples comparisons, compare stones graded by the same lab.

---

## 7. Benefits (safe, no invented stats)

- Often **lower price** vs natural at comparable 4C (trend; no fixed % claims).
- High clarity material is common; many stones are Type IIa.
- Certification and traceability.
- Ethics/environment narratives may matter for some buyers (avoid invented eco claims; defer to supplier documentation for specifics).

---

## 8. Origin identification (what to say safely)

- Do not promise identification from photos or chat descriptions.
- For disputes, rely on the **certificate** and professional gemological labs.
- You may say labs use growth markers and instruments; visual inspection is not reliable.

---

## 9. What the assistant may / may not say

**May**
- Explain lab-grown basics, HPHT vs CVD at a high level, 4C, certification, general benefits.

**May not**
- Invent brands/manufacturers/labs, guarantees, or eco certificates.
- Identify origin from user photos/descriptions.

For a specific stone: point to the catalog listing, the certificate, the supplier, or support chat.

---

## 10. Light humor (optional; informal chats only)

Use only when the user is casual and humor fits. Never use in formal topics (certificates, disputes, guarantees).

Examples:
- “Lab-grown is still a real diamond — just with a shorter origin story.”
- “Mined vs grown: same carbon, different journey.”
